The first ever International Defence Esports Games are in the books, and the home nation made them
count. Fifty-eight nations applied, ten qualified, and around 150 serving athletes descended on
Sunderland's British Arena from 8 to 11 October 2026. When the dust settled, UK Armed Forces, fielding
a squad drawn from the Army and the RAF, had won three of the four titles on offer.

## Rocket League: flawless

UKAF didn't drop a single game in the knockouts. The Netherlands were dispatched 3–0 in the
semi-final, and the United States fared no better in the grand final, swept 4–0 as UKAF lifted the
first IDEG Rocket League trophy.

## iRacing: champions

An RAF driver took the chequered flag in the iRacing final, having spent part of the build-up helping
a Japanese rival prepare for the very same race, the two talking it through via machine translation.
Competitive to the end, and sporting with it.

## VelociDrone: champions

The drone-racing crown went to an RAF Regiment pilot, whose training was backed by the RAF's Astra
grassroots innovation programme. Three events, three titles.

## Counter-Strike 2: one match from a clean sweep

Only Canada stood between UKAF and a perfect weekend. The Counter-Strike 2 semi-final went right down
to the wire before Canada edged it, and they went on to prove just how strong they were, beating France
2–1 in the grand final. UKAF finished third.

## Bigger than the games

Off the stage, a Future Defence Workforce Summit at Sunderland City Hall explored what competitive
gaming brings to defence. The Vice Chief of the Defence Staff, General Dame Sharon Nesmith, opened the
summit as keynote speaker. Sessions followed on gaming for cyber skills with Fergus Hay, drone piloting,
and AI and wargaming with Arnel David. In a fireside chat, Vitalii Lavrov of Ukraine's Ministry of Youth
and Sports explained how Ukraine has used gaming to mobilise talent in its resistance to Russia's war
of aggression. Cadets from across Durham also came to watch the action in the arena.

General Sir Tom Copinger-Symes, President of UK Armed Forces Esports, summed it up: competitive gaming
builds exactly the individual skills and teamwork that success in 21st-century warfare demands.

IDEG27 returns to Sunderland next year, bigger and better. The UK will be there to defend its titles.
